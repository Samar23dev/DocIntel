1. Executive Summary
The train passed automatic signal AS-214 at danger and came to a stop 410 m beyond the signal, after emergency braking, short of the preceding goods train standing on the same line. A rear-end collision was avoided; however, one coach derailed at a trailing crossover. The investigation concluded that the primary cause was loss of signal sighting in dense fog, combined with a non-functional train protection warning on the locomotive.

3. Findings
3.1 Human Factors
The Loco Pilot had completed 7 hours 20 minutes of duty at the time of the incident, within permissible limits.
The Loco Pilot did not reduce speed adequately after sighting the double-yellow aspect in poor visibility.
The Assistant Loco Pilot was not calling out signal aspects as required.
3.2 Equipment
The onboard train protection warning system (TPWS) was found isolated by the crew earlier in the journey after repeated spurious alarms.
Signal AS-214 and its approach aspects were tested and found working correctly.
Brake power tested at 94% at the originating station; emergency braking distance was consistent with the loaded train weight.
3.3 Infrastructure
Signal sighting distance in clear conditions measured at 420 m, compliant with the standard.
Fog signal posts were not provided in this block section.

5. Recommendations
Reinforce fog-season operating instructions: maximum 60 km/h in visibility below 100 m.
Fit all locomotives with a GPS-based fog safety device and verify before departure.
Make TPWS isolation subject to controller approval, with automatic event logging.
Provide fog signal posts at intervals of 1 km on sections with automatic signalling.
Conduct counselling and retraining of the crew on signal call-out discipline.
